## Honest read: sourcing gap or supply gap?

Both, split by what the row is.
- **Where the agency bought a catalog item from a big installed base - PLC modules, two-way radios, actuators, batteries, UPS modules, submersible motors, UV lamps, GC instruments - the part is on the open market right now, usually several times over, at a median 2.3x discount to what the agency paid.** That is a SOURCING gap: the scrapers matched 5 of 6,842 because they searched auction titles for whole units and the dealers that stock these parts sit behind Cloudflare. A human with a search engine found 20 of 51 in about an hour.
- **Where the agency bought an OEM-drawing part - Smith & Loveless impellers and seal kits, Evoqua Tow-Bro headers, JWC bandscreen parts, Flexaseal custom seals, Flowserve rotating assemblies, ZeeWeed membranes, Sensus base-station kits, Radian reference standards, Broen valves by exact catalog string - there is NO secondary market.** That is a SUPPLY gap and no concierge closes it; 24 of 51 rows, and almost every pump and treatment-process row, land here. Pumps: 13 rows, 1 exact hit (a UK dealer) and 1 model-level hit.
- The concierge can deliver on roughly the top third of the index: controls/electrical/radio/instrument catalog parts and common whole units. It cannot deliver on the OEM-only treatment-plant parts that the 'obsolete, 20-week lead time' story is actually about. Flagging the deliverable third as such (by equipment class and by whether the maker's number is a distributor-stocked catalog item) is the honest product.

## Caveats

- Listed prices are asking prices on the day, not transactions; conditions range from new to for-parts, and the agency usually bought new with warranty and installation. The delta is an upper bound on savings.
- One exact hit (#30 Flygt NP 3127.060) is a UK 50 Hz unit; the only US listing had ended.
- Quantity fields are as extracted; where the line was a service contract or 'each', no unit delta was computed.
